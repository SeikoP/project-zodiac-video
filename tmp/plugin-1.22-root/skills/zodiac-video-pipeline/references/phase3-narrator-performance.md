# Phase 3 — Narrator-performance-first writing

Phase 3 writes the narration. It must not feel like a planner narrating its own outline.

## Context boundary

The writer receives a **Phase3WriterBrief**, not the full narrative plan, QC rules, asset library, interaction schema, renderer contract, or package contract.

The brief contains only:
- sign/topic;
- one-sentence thesis;
- 1–3 evidence-safe observable behaviors/tensions;
- 2–4 situation territories;
- narrator stance/vibe;
- optional motif/payoff candidates;
- forbidden unsupported claims.

The planner keeps progression, beat taxonomy, evidence labels, interaction IDs and validation machinery outside the writer prompt. Reattach them only after a complete narration draft exists.

This is deliberate: the writer should perform the story, not satisfy a checklist sentence by sentence.

## Current Conversational vibe

Default for current channel work: **student_peer** unless the user explicitly chooses another vibe.

Student-peer means:
- classmates/friends/group chat/after-school/weekend-plan/crush/game/idol/class debate/homework contexts when they fit naturally;
- young spoken Vietnamese and social reactions;
- short dialogue that sounds like friends talking;
- humor from timing, awkwardness, contradiction and peer reaction.

It does **not** mean mechanically replacing adult nouns:
- sếp → giáo viên;
- công ty → lớp;
- remote → học online.

Choose situations that naturally belong to student/young-friend life.

Avoid workplace-default context such as salary, remote work, KPI, office meetings, resigning, corporate deadlines unless the concept specifically requires it.

## Performance rule

The narrator is inside the telling.

Prefer:
`character acts → another person reacts → narrator notices/catches it → story moves`

Avoid:
`character acts → narrator explains what the action means → narrator restates the trait`

The narrator may:
- interrupt;
- question;
- tease lightly;
- catch a contradiction;
- make one-line side comments;
- call back to something that just happened.

The narrator should sound like a friend watching the behavior happen, not a lecturer translating every scene.

## Show, reframe once, move

After every micro-scene ask:

**Did the viewer already see the point?**

If yes:
- do not paraphrase the scene in 2–3 explanatory sentences;
- use at most one useful reframe, reaction, or bridge;
- then move to the next beat.

Bad:
`scene proves update → “vấn đề là...” → “chỉ là...” → “đó chính là...” → trait restatement`

Better:
`scene proves update → “Ủa, nãy ai nói câu kia?” → next scene`

A later synthesis/payoff may explain the larger pattern once.

## Relational scene depth

When 2+ people are present, the secondary person must alter the situation.

Weak:
- A proposes;
- B supplies a fact;
- A changes answer;
- B supplies another fact.

Stronger:
- A proposes;
- B pushes back;
- A adapts;
- B/group reacts to the adaptation;
- the reaction changes the social state;
- A answers or doubles down.

The second character is not an information dispenser. They are part of the comedy/recognition.

## Keep the script slightly alive

Do not optimize every transition into a clean essay.

Conversational delivery may use:
- abrupt short reactions;
- one-beat pauses;
- a half-step detour;
- an unfinished thought followed by a catch;
- dialogue that exposes the insight before narration names it.

The result should feel controlled but not over-outlined.

## Evidence boundary

Performance can alter framing, fictional dialogue, staging and narrator reaction.

It cannot invent unsupported motive, cause, consequence, frequency, outcome or psychological explanation.

When evidence supports observable behavior but not internal cause, describe what is seen:
- “nghe thêm chi tiết rồi đổi cách nhìn”

not:
- “vì não họ luôn cần cập nhật mọi dữ kiện”.

## Writer self-edit

Before returning Phase 3:
1. read the script aloud;
2. circle every paragraph that sounds like an explanation/article;
3. for each, ask whether a scene/dialogue already proved it;
4. if yes, cut or compress to one reaction/reframe;
5. check that secondary characters actually react;
6. check that student-peer vibe comes from situation + speech, not decorative slang;
7. keep one final synthesis/payoff rather than explaining after every scene.

Do not run Visual Compile from this phase.
