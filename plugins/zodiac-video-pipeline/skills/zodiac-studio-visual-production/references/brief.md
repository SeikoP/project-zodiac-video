# The production brief (read first, then build)

Before any cast, scene or event is chosen, **read the whole script once, as a story**, together with every document the episode carries
(narrative plan, writer brief, episode scope, voice direction, the catalog's vocabulary) and write the brief. Every later choice (cast,
stage, act) is made against the brief, so the episode reads as one argument and not as six unrelated scenes. A brief is the agent's own work
product: it is checked by people, never an approval, and it is not a second source of truth. The narration stays locked.

## What to read
1. **The narration, scene by scene,** and then once more as a whole: what is the claim, how does it turn, where does it land?
2. **The narrative plan** (`story_shape`, `story_spine`, `beats`, `member_lenses`, `audience_vibe`): it is evidence of intent, not a script
   for pictures. Its `story_visual_decision` is Phase 1's opinion; Studio decides its own pictures from the words (see `story-visual.md`).
3. **The writer brief** (premise, narrative engine and motif, second-half pulse, evidence and its boundary, voice direction, exclusions).
   Anything the evidence boundary forbids may not be shown as fact.
4. **The vocabulary:** the frames, mechanics, props, accent parts and picture motifs that exist. The brief names only things that exist.

## What the brief contains (every field is short and concrete)
- `story_read`: two sentences. What the script argues and where it turns.
- `tone`: the audience feeling to keep (warm, teasing, confessional) and what to avoid.
- `characters`: for each cast member, the relationship to the others in one line and what they want in this episode.
- For **every scene**, in order:
  - `beat`: what happens, in plain words, one sentence.
  - `viewer_read`: what a muted viewer must understand (who does what, what changes, what it means).
  - `focus`: the single entity or fact the eye must land on.
  - `setting`: the place implied by what the characters do, never "unspecified".
  - `props`: only physical things the narration handles, each with the action that uses it.
  - `acting_turns`: the two or three moments where someone visibly changes (the words they land on), including the reaction beat.
  - `picture`: one of the picture motifs and the `fact` it makes visible, or `none` with the reason (characters and props already carry it).
- `cover`: the cover is this episode's own, never the concept's template. `topic` (the opening's short theme, begins "Cách" or "Khi", names the
  behaviour of THIS episode, not just the concept), `hook` (6-14 words stating this episode's contradiction or payoff, never the first narration
  line and never cut mid-thought), `scene_id` (the scene whose cast and prop make the cover), `symbol` (the physical object or gesture that makes the
  conflict readable) and `why`. Compare with `recent_episodes`: a topic or hook that reads like one of theirs with another sign's name is rejected.
- `rhythm`: how pictures, settings and shots vary across the episode (no repeated motif, no identical framing in a row).
- `rules_applied`: five rules of this skill you will rely on, each in your own words (a reading receipt, not a quote).
- `risks`: anything the library cannot draw or the script leaves open; a gap is stated, never papered over.

## How the brief is used
The cover entry feeds the opening topic, the cover headline and the publish title. Core hands the whole brief to the cast choice, and the matching scene entry (with `story_read`, `tone`, `rhythm`) to every stage and act choice.
A scene that drops a picture the brief planned is sent back with the brief's own fact. The brief never contains coordinates, asset keys or SVG.
