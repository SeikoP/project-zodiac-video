# Invention: tell the story with people and things, not labels

These are the failures this module exists to prevent. They were found in a finished episode, so look for each in your own plan.
- **Too few people.** The script has two friends, a partner and the lead; the scene shows the lead alone beside a small card that says "1 2".
- **Labels instead of pictures.** A floating card ("ĐẾN MUỘN", a meter, a tally) that would read the same with the characters removed.
- **Idle props.** A phone, a lamp or cups stand in the scene and nothing is done with them.
- **One room, six times.** The same window and lamp, drawn identically, behind every scene.
- **Reuse instead of invention.** The same frame, the same two actors, the same prop family as the last episode.

## 1. Who is on stage
Cast **every person the script gives something to do or something done to** (the lead, the partner, each friend who speaks or is spoken about), one to four members. A person the script names but never shows acting may stay off screen, and the brief says so. Extras may share a body or hair with a lead only when they are visibly different people (outfit, colour, accessory). A scene about "two friends telling their sides" shows both friends; the frame for three people is `three-person-comparison`. Never reduce the cast to make the library easier.

**Capacity.** One frame holds at most three people, and only `three-person-comparison` holds three (it has no prop slot); with a prop in the scene at most two people stand in it. A four-person cast means different scenes show different people; a scene that needs more people than a frame holds is told with the others reacting off-frame (an arm, a voice) or split over two beats.

## 2. Story devices: how a beat is told
Every scene has exactly one **device** in the brief: the visible mechanism by which its beat is told. Pick one below or invent one and describe it in a sentence; "the characters stand and talk" is not a device.
- **Hand-over.** A prop changes hands or owner on a voice anchor (`pass-object`, `offer-object`, `pull-with-prop`): a phone shown to the partner, a gift refused, a note pushed back.
- **Prop as evidence.** The prop's own state carries the news (`phone-ping` becomes `phone-busy` becomes `phone-quiet`; a letter sealed becomes folded). The library has many props with variants; look for them before using a still prop.
- **Distance as meaning.** The gap between people changes with the beat: a step in front to defend, a turn away to sulk, a lean in to listen (`lean` with `dx`, a state swap to a turned pose). Say what the distance means.
- **Accumulation.** The same thing piles up on successive anchors (cups, notes, messages), so a viewer counts without a label.
- **Reaction chain.** A acts, B reacts, C reacts on consecutive anchors; the order is the joke or the point.
- **Split stage.** Two sides each with their own person and prop (`compare-group`, `compare-two-props`), and the lead moves between them.
- **Callback.** A prop, pose or place from an earlier scene returns changed (the phone that interrupted comes back face-down). Name the earlier scene.

## 3. Props must act
A prop is in the scene because a character does something with it **and it changes on screen**: it moves, passes between people, changes state or is revealed. A prop that only stands there is deleted, not kept as decoration. Choose by verb first ("shows the phone to…", "pushes the cup away"), library id second, and prefer an id with state variants. When a needed prop or variant does not exist, record the request (`NEW_VARIANT_MASTER_REQUIRED`); never substitute a still prop.

**Enough props.** Across the episode use at least three different physical props from the library (a scene may have none), each acting in its scene. The cover is dressed with the props the video really uses, so an episode with one prop leaves its cover empty.

## 4. Pictures are about people
A picture (graphic or story visual) makes one fact visible **about what the characters do to each other**. Tests, all of which must pass:
1. **Removal test.** Remove the characters. If the picture still means the same, it is a label, not a story picture: redo it.
2. **Anchor test.** It is revealed on the same voice anchor as a character event; at least one character visibly reads it (turns, points, flinches, smiles).
3. **Change test.** It changes at least once while on screen (a count grows, a side fills, a word is stamped over another).
4. **Own-words test.** Its text is the script's own wording for this moment, not a generic caption ("lý do thông cảm", "kết luận").
5. **Size test.** At phone size a muted viewer reads it without the caption.
A picture is never the only thing in the frame that says who the story is about.

## 5. Settings vary on purpose
Draw the place from what the characters are doing. No two **consecutive** scenes share the same accent set, and no identical set appears in more than two scenes of an episode unless the brief calls it a callback. A drawn set that the library already has is not a reason to reuse it.

## 6. Novelty against recent episodes
The brief receives hints about what recent episodes used (props, frames, picture motifs, sets). Name in `rhythm` what this episode does differently. Reuse is allowed only as a stated callback or when the script demands it.

## 7. Check before you hand in a plan
Count: people on screen vs people in the script; props with a changing event vs props named; pictures that pass all five tests; distinct sets; devices that are not "talking". If any count is short, change the plan, not the wording.
